56.1.0|Paul writes to Titus, whom he left in Crete to set the church in order and to choose elders in every city. Paul lists what such leaders should be like, blameless, hospitable, self-controlled, and holding to sound teaching. He warns about false teachers in Crete who talk too much and mislead families. He says those who profess to know God but deny Him by their works are not pure.||54.3.1-7,44.14.23,59.2.14-17|What qualities do you admire in a good leader?~How do your actions show what you believe?
56.2.0|Paul tells Titus to teach what fits sound doctrine. Older men and women, young men and women, and servants are each given short counsel to live well and be a good example. Paul says the grace of God has appeared to bring salvation to all. It teaches us to leave ungodliness and live soberly, righteously, and godly. Jesus Christ gave Himself to redeem us and to make us a people zealous of good works.||40.5.16,60.2.12,45.8.1|How can you be a good example to someone younger or older than you?~What does it mean to be a people zealous of good works?
56.3.0|Paul reminds Titus to teach people to obey the rulers, to be ready for good works, to speak evil of no one, and to be gentle. He says that all of us were once foolish and lost, but God showed kindness and saved us, not because of our works but by His mercy. This came through the washing of new birth and the renewing of the Holy Ghost.
